# Structured interview feedback

## Problem

Recruiter Labs already helps recruiters understand a candidate before an
interview by evaluating the submitted application against human-confirmed job
criteria.

The product can show:

- which criteria are supported by application evidence;
- which criteria have weak evidence;
- which criteria cannot yet be assessed;
- what should be validated during the interview.

After the interview, however, there is currently no structured way to record
whether those uncertainties were actually resolved.

Important interview observations can therefore remain scattered across memory,
personal notes, messages, or generic comments instead of becoming part of the
candidate's hiring evidence.

This creates a break in the core recruitment loop:

Job
→ confirmed criteria
→ application evidence
→ uncertainty
→ interview
→ missing structured human evidence
→ decision

The interview should close that gap.

## Objective

Turn interviews into structured, attributable human evidence against the job
criteria.

An authorized workspace user should be able to record what an interview
established about the candidate for the criteria that mattered — during the
interview or after it, whichever fits how they work.

Recruiter Labs must preserve a clear distinction between:

1. evidence found in the candidate's submitted application;
2. evidence observed by a human during an interview;
3. the human recruitment decision.

Interview feedback must improve the quality and traceability of human
decision-making without automatically changing AI evaluation or making hiring
decisions.

The core product loop becomes:

Job
→ confirmed criteria
→ application evidence
→ uncertainty
→ interview
→ human evidence
→ human decision

## User behaviour

### Before the interview

The recruiter can already see the candidate's application evaluation and
Interview Brief.

The Interview Brief highlights important criteria that should be validated
because the application contains:

- insufficient evidence;
- weak evidence;
- conflicting evidence;
- another important uncertainty.

This context remains available when preparing for the interview.

### Recording the feedback

An authorized workspace user can record structured interview feedback for any
interview that has not been cancelled, at any point: before it starts, while it
is running, or after it has ended.

Recording as the interview happens is the common case — an interviewer taking
notes live should not have to wait for the slot to end before saving them.

For each relevant job criterion, the interviewer can choose one result:

- **Confirmed**
- **Partially confirmed**
- **Not confirmed**
- **Not assessed**

The interviewer can add a concise evidence note explaining what they observed
during the interview.

Example:

> Criterion: Team leadership
>
> Application evidence: Insufficient evidence
>
> Interview result: Confirmed
>
> Human evidence: Candidate described managing a team of seven engineers,
> including hiring, regular 1:1s and performance reviews.

The goal of the evidence note is to preserve the reason behind the assessment,
not simply the interviewer's conclusion.

The interviewer may also add an optional general interview note for relevant
observations that do not belong cleanly to one criterion.

### Reviewing the candidate

When the application is viewed later, Recruiter Labs clearly separates:

- **Application evidence**
- **Interview evidence**

A recruiter should be able to understand:

- what was known before the interview;
- what was uncertain before the interview;
- what the interviewer observed;
- which uncertainties were resolved;
- which uncertainties still remain;
- who submitted the feedback;
- when the feedback was submitted;
- which interview produced it.

Interview evidence must never look like it was generated by the AI evaluation.

## Feedback result semantics

### Confirmed

The interview produced meaningful human evidence supporting the criterion.

This does not mean the candidate is objectively proven to satisfy the criterion
in every context.

It means the interviewer observed enough relevant evidence during the interview
to consider the criterion supported.

### Partially confirmed

The interview produced some relevant supporting evidence, but important
uncertainty remains.

This is useful when the candidate demonstrated part of the expected capability
but the interviewer could not validate the full criterion.

### Not confirmed

The interview attempted to validate the criterion, but the evidence observed did
not support it sufficiently.

This is different from missing information.

### Not assessed

The criterion was not meaningfully evaluated during this interview.

This must remain different from `Not confirmed`.

Failure to ask about something is not negative candidate evidence.

## Business rules

The existing product principles for recruitment workflow and evaluation
integrity remain authoritative.

### Human evidence is separate from AI evidence

Interview feedback is explicitly human-authored evidence.

Recruiter Labs must never present interview feedback as if the AI generated,
verified, or independently confirmed it.

### Interview feedback does not rewrite application evaluation

The candidate's existing application evaluation represents the evidence
available from the application at that point in time.

Submitting interview feedback must not silently recalculate or overwrite:

- application fit;
- evidence coverage;
- criterion application scores;
- criterion application confidence;
- application evidence;
- the original Interview Brief.

The historical pre-interview evaluation must remain understandable.

### Unknown remains different from negative evidence

If a criterion was unknown before the interview and is not assessed during the
interview, it remains unresolved.

`Not assessed` must never be interpreted as:

- Not confirmed;
- low fit;
- failure;
- negative evidence.

### Human evidence must have a reason

For results that make a meaningful assertion about the criterion, the product
should encourage the interviewer to preserve the evidence behind that
assessment.

The product should prioritize statements such as:

> Candidate described operating PostgreSQL databases at approximately 500GB and
> explained their backup and replication strategy.

over conclusions such as:

> Seems very experienced.

The product does not need to police the quality of human writing automatically,
but the interface should encourage evidence rather than unsupported opinion.

### Feedback is attributable

Every submitted interview feedback record must preserve:

- the human author;
- the interview;
- the candidate application;
- the submission time.

The product must never make human feedback appear anonymous unless anonymity is
explicitly introduced by a future feature.

### Feedback belongs to the relevant job

Interview feedback about a criterion must belong to the job for which the
candidate was interviewed.

Evidence from one hiring process must not silently become evidence for another
job.

### Historical meaning must be preserved

If the job criteria change after interview feedback has been submitted, the
historical feedback must remain understandable in the context in which it was
recorded.

Later edits to the job must not silently rewrite what the interviewer originally
evaluated.

### Cancelled interviews do not produce completed-interview evidence

A cancelled interview cannot receive normal completed-interview feedback.

### Feedback belongs to an interview, and says when it was written

Structured interview feedback is always attached to a specific interview, and
the product never hides when it was written relative to that interview.

Timing does not gate recording. An interviewer may save what they observed
before the slot ends, and a note taken live is worth more than one reconstructed
from memory hours later.

Timing does change how the feedback reads. Feedback recorded while the interview
has not yet ended is shown as what it is — written before the interview
finished — so a reader can never mistake a pre-interview expectation for an
observation the interview actually produced. The same applies when an interview
is later cancelled or moved: the observation stands, and its context is stated.

The product does not police what an interviewer writes and when. It does refuse
to present a note as something it is not.

### No automatic recruitment decision

Submitting interview feedback must never automatically:

- advance the candidate;
- reject the candidate;
- hire the candidate;
- move the candidate to another pipeline stage;
- close the application.

Those remain explicit human workflow actions.

### No automatic AI reinterpretation

Submitting interview feedback must not automatically trigger AI to reinterpret
the candidate, generate a new fit score, or produce a final hiring
recommendation.

Human interview evidence remains a separate decision-support layer.

### Source and referral remain irrelevant

Application source, referral status and sourcing provenance must not affect
interview feedback semantics.

A referred candidate is assessed using the same interview evidence model as any
other candidate.

## User flow

### 1. Candidate reaches interview

The candidate has progressed through the recruitment process and an interview is
scheduled.

The recruiter can use the existing application evaluation and Interview Brief as
preparation context.

### 2. Interview takes place

The interviewer conducts the interview using their normal interviewing process.

Recruiter Labs does not need to record, transcribe or listen to the interview for
this feature.

### 3. Interview becomes eligible for feedback

Recruiter Labs provides a clear action to record interview feedback on any
interview that has not been cancelled, whether it is upcoming, running or
finished.

Feedback saved before the interview has ended is labelled as such wherever it is
later reviewed.

### 4. Interviewer records criterion evidence

For each criterion they meaningfully assessed, the interviewer records:

- Confirmed;
- Partially confirmed;
- Not confirmed;
- Not assessed.

Where relevant, they add a concise evidence note.

Criteria that were important uncertainties before the interview should be easy
to recognize.

### 5. Interviewer submits feedback

The feedback becomes part of the candidate's recruitment record.

The product records the author and submission time.

### 6. Recruiter reviews the evidence

The application now exposes two distinct evidence layers:

#### Application evidence

What Recruiter Labs could infer from the candidate's submitted application.

#### Interview evidence

What a human interviewer observed during one or more interviews.

The recruiter can see where interview evidence:

- confirmed previous evidence;
- added evidence where none existed before;
- contradicted or weakened an earlier assumption;
- only partially resolved uncertainty;
- left uncertainty unresolved.

### 7. Human decides what happens next

The recruiter or hiring team uses all available information to make the next
workflow decision.

Recruiter Labs provides evidence and context.

It does not make that decision automatically.

## Multiple interviews

A candidate may participate in more than one interview.

Each interview must preserve its own feedback rather than collapsing all
interview observations into one mutable candidate score.

The application should be able to show evidence chronologically and attribute it
to the interview and author that produced it.

Two interviewers may legitimately reach different observations.

The product should preserve both rather than silently choosing one as the
"correct" answer.

Reconciling disagreement remains a human hiring-team responsibility.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC01** — An authorized workspace user can record structured feedback for an
  interview that has not been cancelled, whether it is upcoming, running or
  finished.

- **AC02** — Interview feedback can record `Confirmed`, `Partially confirmed`,
  `Not confirmed`, or `Not assessed` for a relevant job criterion.

- **AC03** — `Not assessed` remains semantically different from `Not confirmed`
  and does not create negative candidate evidence.

- **AC04** — An interviewer can add a human-authored evidence note to a criterion
  result.

- **AC05** — An interviewer can add an optional general interview note.

- **AC06** — Submitted feedback preserves its human author, interview, candidate
  application and submission time.

- **AC07** — Feedback for a criterion cannot belong to a different job or
  workspace from the interview application.

- **AC08** — A cancelled interview cannot receive normal completed-interview
  feedback.

- **AC09** — Recording is never gated on timing, and feedback recorded before the
  interview has ended is visibly marked as written before the interview
  finished, so it cannot be read as an observation the interview produced.

- **AC10** — Application evidence and human interview evidence are visibly and
  semantically separate when reviewing a candidate.

- **AC11** — Submitting interview feedback does not overwrite or automatically
  recalculate the existing AI application fit.

- **AC12** — Submitting interview feedback does not automatically change evidence
  coverage from the original application evaluation.

- **AC13** — Submitting interview feedback does not automatically move, reject,
  hire or otherwise change the candidate's recruitment outcome.

- **AC14** — A criterion that was previously unknown can later show human
  interview evidence without rewriting the fact that it was unknown from the
  original application.

- **AC15** — A criterion marked `Not assessed` remains visibly unresolved rather
  than becoming a failed criterion.

- **AC16** — Historical interview feedback remains understandable if the job
  criteria are edited later.

- **AC17** — Multiple interviews can preserve independent feedback records for the
  same candidate application.

- **AC18** — Feedback from different interviewers can coexist without one
  automatically replacing or overriding the other.

- **AC19** — Existing Interview Brief guidance remains recognizable as
  pre-interview preparation and is not presented as human interview feedback.

- **AC20** — Referral or application source does not change interview feedback
  semantics.

## Edge cases

### Interview happened but a criterion was not discussed

Record the criterion as `Not assessed` when appropriate.

Do not infer failure.

### Interviewer could only partially validate a criterion

Use `Partially confirmed`.

Do not force a binary positive/negative answer.

### Interviewer disagrees with the application evaluation

Preserve both.

The product should show the original application evidence and the later human
interview evidence.

It should not silently rewrite history to make the two agree.

### Two interviewers disagree

Preserve both attributed observations.

The product should not automatically average, vote on, or resolve the
disagreement.

### Job criteria change later

Existing feedback remains historical evidence from the criterion context that
was valid at the time of the interview.

### Candidate has several interviews

Each interview keeps its own structured feedback and attribution.

The candidate/application view may aggregate presentation for readability, but
the underlying human evidence must remain traceable to its source interview.

## Out of scope

This feature deliberately does not include:

- interview audio recording;
- interview video recording;
- transcription;
- AI notetaker;
- AI-generated interview notes;
- AI-generated criterion feedback;
- sentiment analysis;
- facial, voice or emotion analysis;
- automatic post-interview fit recalculation;
- a combined AI + human candidate score;
- automatic hiring recommendations;
- automatic rejection;
- automatic pipeline movement;
- automatic hiring decisions;
- interviewer performance scoring;
- interviewer calibration analytics;
- bias analytics;
- complex interview-template builders;
- interview panels and advanced panel orchestration;
- enterprise approval workflows;
- full workspace role/RBAC redesign.

These may be considered separately only when there is product evidence that they
solve a meaningful customer problem.
